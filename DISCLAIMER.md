## What this software actually does, in plain terms

You are not installing a linter. Understand these before you enable it:

1. **It invokes a separate autonomous AI agent** and gives it the staged changes and files from
   the repository.
2. **On the Codex backend it executes commands in the repository**, including the repository's
   test suite, in a sandbox with approvals disabled. Command execution can modify or delete files, consume
   resources, and reach the network. A repository you do not trust becomes a code-execution
   vector against your machine.
3. **It sends your code to a third-party AI provider** under your own credentials, subject to
   that provider's terms and data handling. See [`PRIVACY.md`](PRIVACY.md).
4. **Its verdicts are model output, and can be wrong in both directions** — approving a change
   with defects, security flaws, or licence problems, and rejecting a correct one.
5. **It can be manipulated by the content it reads.** A crafted diff, dependency, or file is a
   prompt-injection vector against the reviewer.
6. **It writes state, logs and retained verdicts to your machine in plaintext**, including
   quoted excerpts of the code it reviewed.
7. **It may direct another AI agent to act.** `reviewerAgent` and `stopContinue` can generate
   verdicts, recommendations, answers, permissions, authorizations and imperative directions that
   a developer agent may follow without further human confirmation. Depending on that agent's own
   tools, credentials and permissions, resulting actions may include modifying or deleting files;
   staging, committing, pushing or merging code; running commands or tests; installing software;
   accessing networks or third-party services; starting or stopping computing resources;
   deploying or publishing software; communicating with third parties; disclosing information; or
   incurring charges. The model may misunderstand the user's instructions or incorrectly conclude
   that an action was previously authorized.
8. **The bundled Claude Code slash commands intentionally use `!` shell preprocessing.** They are
   marked for manual invocation only, but their arguments are processed by the shell. Avoid shell
   syntax, and do not paste untrusted text into `/reviewerDirective`, which accepts free-form text.

## Your responsibilities

Users are responsible for:

- **Backups and version control.** Keep recoverable copies of anything you cannot afford to lose,
  independent of this tool.
- **Reviewing what it does.** Read its verdicts and the changes it influenced before relying on
  them.
- **Autonomous directions and permissions.** Decide whether to enable autonomous continuation;
  limit the developer agent's tools, credentials and external access; use repository and provider
  protections; and review any action that could be destructive, costly, externally visible or
  irreversible.
- **Slash-command arguments.** Invoke the bundled Claude Code commands manually and provide only
  expected, trusted literal arguments.
- **Where you point it.** Do not enable it in repositories whose contents you do not trust.
- **Least privilege.** Run it under an account with only the access it needs.
- **Your provider accounts.** All usage, quota, and charges incurred with any AI provider.
- **Compliance.** Whether using it is permitted by your employer, your clients, your provider's
